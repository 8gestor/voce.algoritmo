## c12-002
Technofeudalism:<br>The End of Capitalism

## c12-003
If you have an Instagram account with 10,000 followers, you feel like a "creator." But the economic reality is different: you're a feudal serf. The lord of the castle (Meta) lets you farm the land (post photos, create content), but he keeps the entire harvest (the data and the ad revenue) and can throw you out at any moment without notice and without a right to defend yourself. We no longer live in a digital free-market capitalism; we live, as the Greek economist <strong>Yanis Varoufakis</strong> puts it, in <strong>Technofeudalism</strong>.

## c12-004
<span class="thesis-header">// The Death of Profit, the Rise of Rent</span> "Varoufakis argues that traditional capitalism (based on profit from producing goods) has been replaced by an economy of <strong>Feudal Rent</strong>. Amazon doesn't produce the goods; it charges a 'toll' for sellers and buyers to meet. Google doesn't produce the content; it charges a toll for you to find the information. Power has migrated from the industrialists to the owners of <strong>Cloud Capital</strong>. Whoever owns the servers and the algorithms acts like a medieval landlord: he doesn't innovate, he extracts tribute from everyone who steps on his digital soil."

## c12-005
The Anatomy of Enshittification

## c12-006
The activist and author <strong>Cory Doctorow</strong> described with surgical precision how platforms die. He calls the process <strong>Enshittification</strong>. It isn't an accident; it's the business model. Platforms follow a predictable cycle of betrayal:

## c12-007
<span style="display:block; text-align:center; font-family:var(--font-tech); font-weight:bold; color:var(--c-gold); margin-bottom:15px;">THE PLATFORM DEGRADATION CYCLE</span> <div class="cycle-step step-1"> <span class="step-title">PHASE 1: THE SUBSIDY ("Welcome!")</span> The platform operates at a loss to attract users. The service is amazing, with no ads, Uber is cheap, Google has no spam. The goal is to create the "Network Effect" and trap you (Lock-in). </div> <div class="arrow-down">↓</div> <div class="cycle-step step-2"> <span class="step-title">PHASE 2: THE CAPTURE ("Pay the Toll")</span> With users trapped, the platform focuses on business customers. Organic reach drops. You have to pay to reach your own followers. Uber gets expensive, Amazon prioritizes sponsored products. </div> <div class="arrow-down">↓</div> <div class="cycle-step step-3"> <span class="step-title">PHASE 3: TOTAL EXTRACTION ("Scorched Earth")</span> With users and businesses both trapped (high exit cost), the platform extracts every last cent. The service becomes horrible for both. The feed is nothing but ads and AI junk. The goal is maximum profit before the final collapse. </div>

## c12-008
Enshittification has dates. In June 2023, Reddit began charging for access to its API; the developer of Apollo, the most popular third-party app, estimated the new fee at about 20 million dollars a year and shut the app down, and more than 8,000 communities went dark or private in protest. Reddit said it wanted to charge those who use its data to train AI.

## c12-009
Chokepoint Capitalism: The Toll on Innovation

## c12-010
The result of this process is <strong>Chokepoint Capitalism</strong>. Big Tech creates artificial chokepoints between creators and audiences. Apple charges up to 30% of digital sales made through the App Store (with lower rates for small developers and recent changes under regulatory pressure). Spotify pays musicians fractions of a cent per play (something like US$0.003 to 0.005, according to industry estimates). They place themselves in the middle of every human transaction and charge a toll.

## c12-011
This destroys innovation. Startups can't compete because they have to pay the "feudal tax" just to exist in the app stores. The digital market has become a set of fortified castles (Walled Gardens) where interoperability is forbidden. For years you couldn't send a WhatsApp message to another app, not because of a technical limitation, but because of a commercial decision to keep the castle's moat impassable. The law has started to move on this: the European Union's Digital Markets Act (DMA) forced WhatsApp to open up messaging with third-party apps, which began rolling out in Europe in November 2025, initially for one-to-one chats.

## c12-012
Antithesis: The Return to the Commons

## c12-013
The only way to break feudalism is to take back ownership of the land. Historically, there were the <strong>Commons</strong>, managed by the community. In the digital world, that means adopting <strong>Open Protocols</strong> instead of Closed Platforms.

## c12-014
Email is a protocol: you can send an email from Gmail to Outlook. Nobody "owns" email. The Fediverse (Mastodon, Bluesky, Lemmy) tries to revive that logic. If you don't like a server's rules, you move to another one, taking your followers and your data with you. It's <strong>Digital Land Reform</strong>.

## c12-015
DIGITAL FIEF (Instagram/TikTok)

## c12-016
Centralized. The owner dictates the rules. If your account is banned, you lose everything (identity and connections). An algorithm decides what you see. You're the serf.

## c12-017
THE COMMONS (Fediverse/Web)

## c12-018
Decentralized. Community rules. Guaranteed data portability (you take your followers with you). You choose the chronological feed. You're the citizen.

## c12-019
Who Controls the Chokepoints (and How They Open)

## c12-020
Varoufakis's thesis (<em>Technofeudalism</em>, 2023) is provocative and controversial. Economists note that big tech still earns profits from products and advertising, and that monopoly "rent" has always existed under capitalism; the debate is whether the scale of the platforms changes the nature of the system. The chapter's most solid concept is <em>enshittification</em>, from Cory Doctorow (2022): an observable pattern in the life cycle of platforms. The American Dialect Society chose the word as its 2023 "Word of the Year."

## c12-021
There are examples of forced opening: the DMA (2024) imposes interoperability and bans self-preferencing by the "gatekeepers," and Apple had to allow alternative app stores in the European Union. There are also open protocols on the rise: email, the web, RSS and, more recently, ActivityPub (the Fediverse) and Bluesky's AT Protocol.

## c12-022
But openness has limits that are worth admitting: decentralized networks suffer from uneven moderation, hosting costs and low adoption. Digital "land reform" depends on funding models (platform cooperatives, subscriptions, public support), and not just on protocols.

## c12-023
Synthesis: The POSSE Protocol

## c12-024
The strategy of tactical resistance has a name: <strong>POSSE</strong> (Publish on your Own Site, Syndicate Elsewhere).

## c12-025
Never create original content directly on the feudal lord's platform. Create it on your blog, on your site, in your newsletter. That is your sovereign territory. Then share the link on social media. If Instagram dies tomorrow or if Elon Musk decides to charge for Twitter, your archive and your history stay intact on your own domain. The social network should be only a distribution channel, never your home.

## c12-026
If platforms are fiefdoms, what alternatives exist? The next chapter is about imagining, and building, other ones.
